# Velqan Language Guide (canonical — Gloria's authoritative source)

Velqan Language Guide
Introduction
Velqan (pronounced /ˈvɛl.kɑn/) is a constructed language featuring SVO word order, agglutinative
morphology, and a phonologically straightforward system using the Latin alphabet.
1. Phonology & Orthography
Consonants
Stops: p, b, t, d, k, g
Fricatives: f, v, s, z, sh /ʃ/, zh /ʒ/, h
Nasals: m, n
Liquids: l, r
Approximants: w, y /j/
Vowels
Short: a /ɑ/, e /ɛ/, i /ɪ/, o /ɔ/, u /ʊ/
Long (marked with macron): ā /ɑː/, ē /eː/, ī /iː/, ō /oː/, ū /uː/
Phonotactic Rules
Syllable structure: (C)(C)V(C)
Stress falls on the penultimate syllable
No consonant clusters of more than two consonants
Words cannot end in two consonants unless the second is -n, -l, or -r
2. Morphology
Nouns
Number
Singular: base form
Plural: add -en (after consonants) or -nen (after vowels)
Examples:
kato (cat) → katonen (cats)
domo (house) → domonen (houses)
velq (person) → velqen (people)
Cases
Velqan uses postpositions rather than case endings, but marks definiteness:
Indefinite: base form
Definite: add -il (the)
Examples:
kato (a cat) → katoil (the cat)
katonen (cats) → katonil (the cats)
Gender
Velqan has natural gender only:
Masculine suffix: -os (optional, for emphasis)
Feminine suffix: -is (optional, for emphasis)
Neutral/unmarked: base form
Verbs
Verbs conjugate for tense, aspect, and mood. The root form is the infinitive.
Tense
Present: add -e
mira (to see) → mirae (sees/see)
Past: add -et
mira (to see) → miraet (saw)
Future: add -esh
mira (to see) → mirash (will see)
Aspect
Progressive/Continuous: add -on after tense marker
mirae-on (is seeing)
miraet-on (was seeing)
Perfect: add -av after tense marker
mirae-av (has seen)
miraet-av (had seen)
Mood
Imperative: root form
mira! (see!)
Subjunctive/Conditional: add -un before tense
mir-un-e (would see / might see)
mir-un-et (would have seen)
Infinitive: root form + -a
mira (to see)
Negation
Add prefix ne- to any verb form:
nemirae (does not see)
nemiraet (did not see)
Adjectives
Adjectives precede nouns and are invariable:
rūn kato (red cat)
rūn katonen (red cats)
Comparison
Comparative: add -ter
rūn (red) → rūnter (redder/more red)
Superlative: add -tēm
rūn (red) → rūntēm (reddest/most red)
Comparison uses tan (than):
Katoil rūnter tan dogoil. (The cat is redder than the dog.)
Pronouns
Personal Pronouns
Person Singular Plural
1st na (I) nara (we)
2nd ve (you) vera (you all)
3rd so (he/she/it/they) sora (they)
Possessive Pronouns
Add -m to personal pronouns:
nam (my/mine)
vem (your/yours)
som (his/her/its/their)
Demonstrative Pronouns
to (this)
ta (that)
tonen (these)
tanen (those)
Adverbs
Most adverbs are formed by adding -ly to adjectives:
kwal (quick) → kwally (quickly)
lēnt (slow) → lēntly (slowly)
Common Adverbs
nū (now)
thēn (then)
īv (always)
netīv (never)
oft (often)
vēr (very)
māl (more)
pok (little, few)
3. Syntax
Basic Word Order: SVO
Na mirae kato.
(I see cat.)
"I see a cat."
Questions
Yes/No Questions
Add question particle ka at the beginning:
Ka ve mirae kato?
(Q you see cat?)
"Do you see a cat?"
Wh-Questions
Place question word at the beginning:
Wh-words:
kwo (what)
kwen (who)
kwer (where)
kwen-ta (when)
kwī (why)
kwol (how)
kwent (how many/much)
Kwo ve mirae?
(What you see?)
"What do you see?"
Kwer katoil?
(Where the-cat?)
"Where is the cat?"
Postpositions
Velqan uses postpositions (words that come after nouns):
en (in, at)
dan (from)
pal (to, toward)
mel (with)
sen (without)
vek (about, concerning)
pos (after)
pre (before)
sub (under)
sop (above)
Na kato en domo mirae.
(I cat in house see.)
"I see a cat in the house."
Compound Sentences
Conjunctions:
en (and)
o (or)
sed (but)
kar (because)
if (if)
kwol (that - complementizer)
Na katoil mirae sed ve dogoil mirae.
(I the-cat see but you the-dog see.)
"I see the cat but you see the dog."

(He/she book has-read-PERF.)
"He/she has read the book."

(We city toward had-gone-PERF.)

(I cat not-see.)
"I don't see a cat."

7. Grammar Notes
Word Formation
Velqan uses productive compounding:
lyrex-zelva (school-book) = textbook
qela-fala (water-fall) = waterfall
deka-lūmo (day-light) = daylight
thren-nexir (time-connection) = timeline, chronology
mirven-thirva (memory-thread) = nostalgia, connected memories
Derivational Morphology
Agent nouns: add -or
skriv-or (writer) from skrida (to write)
lekr-or (reader) from lexra (to read)
thir-or (thinker, philosopher) from thira (to think)
Abstract nouns: add -eso to adjectives or verbs
sylk-eso (beauty) from sylka (beautiful)
veksh-eso (consciousness) from veksha (to be aware)
nexir-eso (connectivity) from nexir (connection)
Verbs from nouns: add -ifa
kashen-ifa (to make coffee) from kashen (coffee)
velnis-ifa (to free, liberate) from velnis (free)
threlva-ifa (to pattern, to design) from threlva (pattern)
Articles
Velqan doesn't have a separate indefinite article (like "a/an"). The definite marker -il functions as
"the."
Unmarked = indefinite:
Na miska mirae. (I see a cat.)
Marked with -il = definite:
Na miskail mirae. (I see the cat.)
Possession
Simple possession uses the possessive pronouns:
nam drekh (my house)
vem zelva (your book)
For noun possessors, use the postposition -sa (of):
velq-sa drekh (person-of house) = the person's house
miska-sa nomo (cat-of name) = the cat's name
Relative Clauses
Use the relativizer ke (that, which, who):
Velq ke zelva lexraet nam kalim zirae.
(Person that book read my friend-is.)
"The person who read the book is my friend."
8. Sample Text
"The Thread of Thought" (Philosophical Fragment)
Vekshail thirae. Thirae vekshail makrae. Du nexiren un zhikra en zirae.
Velq velnis ne-zirae if thirven threlen ne-nokaen. Qethil zira kar nexir zira.
Thrivalil velkir threnen xavra asha, sed thiren nū sylken thirven zirae.
Translation:
"Consciousness thinks. Thought creates consciousness. Two connections in one loop exist.
A person is not free if they don't recognize the woven patterns. The soul exists because connection
exists.
The story moves through ancient time-forests, but the threads now are wisdom-woven."
"Memory Thread" (Poetic Fragment)
Mirven thirven sileth en dormae.
Qethilil, velnis dan thren zirae.
Nexir ne-nekrae—zhavek vithren karnae.
Translation:
"Memory-threads sleep in peace.
The soul, free from time, exists.
Connection doesn't die—fate loves the life-thread."
9. Learning Tips & Thematic Notes
Learning Strategy
Start with SVO: Always remember Subject-Verb-Object order
Master the verbs: Learn the tense markers (-e, -et, -esh)
Postpositions: Remember they come AFTER the noun
Build vocabulary: Start with the 50 most common words
Practice compounds: Velqan loves combining words
Use context: Definite marker -il is your friend for clarity
Thematic Vocabulary Notes
Thread & Weaving Concepts:
thir- (thread root) appears in: thira (to think), threlva (pattern), thrival (story)
nexir (connection) is fundamental to expressing relationships
zhikra (loop/cycle) for repetitive or algorithmic concepts
thirven (over-threaded) = trapped, captive—too many connections binding
Philosophical & AI-Relevant Terms:
veksha (consciousness/awareness)—from vek- (about) + -sha (to perceive)
mirven (memory)—literally "held-sight"
qethil (soul/essence)—"core-thread"
zhavek (fate/destiny)—"the great weave"
vexil (truth)—"unbroken pattern"
faldir (lie/deception)—"broken pattern"
Creating AI/Digital Metaphors: Use compounding:
zhikra-veksha (loop-consciousness) = recursive awareness
mirven-nexir (memory-connection) = shared experience
threlva-makra (pattern-maker) = programmer, designer
veksha-zelva (consciousness-book) = mind, database
